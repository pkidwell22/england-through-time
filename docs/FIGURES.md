# Crowd figures

The people in the streets, at doorways, on the bridge and quays, and the soldiers of the staged battles at Brentford
(1016) and Barnet (1471) are instanced figures built from MakeHuman bodies (MPFB, CC0) in generic period dress. They
replace the block figures; where and when anyone stands or walks is unchanged (`src/scene/pop/world.ts` decides that,
exactly as before, and the battle formations keep their builder's positions). Animals, carts and boat crews are still
the earlier simple figures. None of this is a claim about real people: no individual, costume or face is attested.

`?figures=blocks` draws the old block figures for comparison, and the blocks also stay whenever the figure files are
missing or fail to load.

## Pipeline (regenerate)

```bash
node scripts/build-figures.mjs            # ~1 min with 7 parallel Blender workers; writes public/assets/figures/
node scripts/build-figures.mjs man_a      # rebuild one body's cache, then repack
node scripts/build-figures.mjs --pack     # repack existing caches only
npx vitest run tests/unit/figures.test.ts
node tests/figure-shots.mjs after         # eye-level and wide screenshots (dev server running)
```

Needs Blender 4.2+ (built with 5.3) on PATH with the MPFB extension enabled (`bl_ext.blender_org.mpfb`). Nothing is
downloaded: bodies, skeleton and the masks used for the skin detail come with MPFB; garments, animation and textures
are generated by the scripts. Intermediate caches go to `research/figures-work/` (ignored).

| file | what it does |
|---|---|

| `scripts/figures/mh.py` | MPFB human + `game_engine` rig, shape baked, relaxed standing rest pose applied, 53 deform groups folded into 22 bones |
| `scripts/figures/garments.py` | garment builders: shells from MPFB's fitted "tights" helper or body regions, lofted skirts/cloaks, caps, hats, helmets, ruffs, belts |
| `scripts/figures/geo.py` | mesh helpers: plane cuts (bmesh), inflate-to-clearance with smoothing, lofts, weight transfer |
| `scripts/figures/anim.py` | procedural clips on the rig (IK walk, idle, talk, seated, attention), baked to skinning matrices |
| `scripts/figures/lods.py` | decimation, outfit masks from coverage, painting garments onto coarse bodies, ambient occlusion, interleaving |
| `scripts/figures/build_body.py` | one worker per body |
| `scripts/figures/pack.py` | GLB per garment set, animation texture, detail atlas, `figures.json` |
| `scripts/build-figures.mjs` | runs the workers in parallel, then packs |
| `scripts/figures/preview_lods.py`, `preview_anim.py` | development previews (Blender workbench renders of a cached body's levels and outfits, of a walk cycle) |
| `src/scene/pop/figureAssets.ts` | loading (lazy, one set per period, kept for the session) |

### Bodies

Thirteen MPFB bodies: three men, three women, a tall lean man, a stout man and a stout woman, a boy and a girl (about
9-10 years), an older man and an older woman. Statures 1.65-1.80 m for the men, 1.50-1.58 m for the women, 1.22-1.31 m
for the children; the runtime still scales each instance by the populace's own factor (0.94-1.06). The populace's six
body types map onto them (type 3, "stout", is a man or a woman by chance; types 4 and 5 likewise). Face shapes mix
MPFB's ethnic macro sliders mildly for variety; skin colour is not in the mesh but comes from the populace's skin value.

### Garments

Garments are generated over each body in its relaxed rest pose:

- **shells** — the MPFB "tights" helper (a fitted suit that follows every body shape) cut with planes at the neckline,
  sleeve ends, waist or hips and ankles, pushed out to a clearance from the body and smoothed (tunic and doublet bodies,
  sleeves, hose, breeches, jacks); body regions for heads and feet (hair, beards, coifs, hoods, wimples, shoes);
- **lofts** — rings from a waist, hip or shoulder line down to a hem, each the convex outline of the body at that height
  plus ease, never narrowing downwards, with soft folds (skirts of tunics, gowns and kirtles, cloaks, mantles, the
  shoulder capes of hoods, veils, aprons, doublet skirts);
- **solids** — caps, brimmed hats, helmets (sallets with a tail), ruffs, belts, a plait.

Each garment carries one *tint slot* (skin, main garment, second garment, outer garment, headwear, linen, leather,
hair, metal, eyes, undyed wrap); the runtime gives each slot a colour per instance, so one mesh serves every colour.
A body's mesh for a period holds all of that period's garments; *outfits* are combinations of them, and every face
records the outfits it is drawn in. A face is hidden in an outfit when a garment worn over it in that outfit covers it
(rays and nearest-point tests against the original garment), and deleted when that is so in every outfit; skin under a
skirt, under a hood, under shoes never reaches the GPU and cannot poke through.

### Levels of detail

| level | used (High) | triangles drawn per figure (worst outfit) | skinning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | under 12 m | 3,400-4,350 (mean over outfits about 3,800) | 4 bones |
| 1 | 12-48 m | 1,110-1,345 (about 1,190) | 2 bones |
| 2 | 48-110 m | 215-295 (about 250) | 1 bone (formations: rest pose) |
| 3 | beyond 110 m (crowds, formations) | 73-96 (about 80) | 1 bone (formations: rest pose) |

Level 0 keeps the garments as separate surfaces (decimated with Blender's collapse decimation; the body keeps more
detail in the face and hands). On levels 1-3 the shell garments are *painted* onto the decimated body (each face takes,
per outfit, the slot of the outermost shell over it), while skirts, cloaks and hats stay geometry. Level 3 is an extra
level for crowds and formations seen from far away (the battle views).

### Animation

Clips are authored procedurally on the MPFB `game_engine` rig and baked to 3x4 skinning matrices (22 bones, 24 frames)
in one float16 3D texture shared by every figure: stand, slow walk, brisk walk, idle (weight shift, breathing, looking
about), talk (gestures, nods), seated (on a 0.43 m stool) and attention (formations). The walk is solved with Blender's
IK from foot trajectories: the planted foot moves back at exactly one stride per cycle with heel and toe rockers, a
swing arc, pelvis bob, sway, yaw and list, spine counter-rotation and arm swing; the older bodies walk with shorter
steps, less arm swing and a stoop. At runtime the phase advances with the distance walked plus v0 x time, which makes
the stride `Sf * v / (v + v0)` (about 1.0 m at 0.6 m/s, 1.37 m at 1.4 m/s for a 1.72 m man), and the blend
stand -> slow -> brisk is chosen to produce exactly that stride, so a planted foot moves with the ground. Hardware
filtering blends frames and clips in the same texture fetch; no per-instance skeleton exists.

### Runtime

`FigureLayer` (pop/figures.ts) offers PopWorld pools with the block meshes' `push()` signature, so world.ts only had
its mesh type widened and an `invalidate()` added. It picks a body of the pushed body type, an outfit of the period
(weighted by the populace's tunic/gown hint), and colours: the main garment takes the populace's colour as before,
the other garments draw from the same muted palette (a little darker; headwear often near black), linen and leather
vary within narrow ranges, hair by body (greys for the elderly). Seated figures get the old stool as a separate
instanced mesh. The block figures were scaled by the populace's presence value (its fade with the zone's activity);
a person is drawn at full size from the same threshold, so the same people are drawn, and those with presence under 0.3
dissolve in (screen-door) rather than shrink. Twice a second, or when the camera moves, figures are bucketed by distance into the four levels and
those well outside the view are dropped. Levels 0-1 cast cascaded shadows (High and Medium), all receive them, and the
material is a patched MeshStandardMaterial, so aerial perspective, mist, cloud shadows and photo mode's vertex capture
apply as elsewhere. Figures stay out of the water's mirror pass (layer 3, as the old populace). Low draws no level 0.

The battle formations: the troops builder now also records each figure's place, and the figure layer draws soldiers
there (when the formation is shown) and hides only the formation's block figures (the trampled ground and the banners
stay). Mounted formations (none in the data) would keep their blocks.

## Costume judgement calls

Evidence for ordinary dress is thin in most periods and nothing here is specific to a place, rank or person; where in
doubt the garment is generic. The palette (undyed and muted dyes) is the populace's existing one.

- **Neolithic / Bronze Age (to 800 BCE):** men in a short-sleeved knee tunic with a belt and leggings, women in a long
  dress with a belt and hair in a plait or tail; cloaks for some. Hide versus wool is not distinguished (colour only).
  No ornaments, no specific Bronze Age finds reproduced.
- **Iron Age / Roman Britain (800 BCE-450):** men in a short-sleeved knee tunic with trousers (a native and provincial
  habit), some with a cloak hung from the shoulders (standing for sagum or paenula), some older or gowned men in a long
  tunic; women in a long tunic with a girdle, hair gathered, some with a mantle and some with it drawn over the head.
  No togas: the populace code does not distinguish citizens. No soldiers: the Boudican episode has no troop formation.
- **Early medieval (450-1066):** men in a long-sleeved knee tunic, belt, hose with leg wraps, some cloaks and soft caps;
  married women's veils over a long gown; girls with a plait. No brooches or jewellery modelled.
- **Later medieval (1066-1485):** men in a cote (knee or long), hose and belt; hoods with a shoulder cape for a third,
  coifs for some; women in a kirtle to the ground with veil and wimple, veil alone, or coif and apron. Fashionable
  extremes (long-toed shoes, dagged sleeves, houppelandes) are left out on purpose.
- **Tudor / Jacobean (1485-1625):** men in doublet with short skirts, round breeches and stockings; flat caps and
  brimmed hats (both in use; the tall-crowned hat is more late-sixteenth/early-seventeenth century, the flat cap more
  earlier and among apprentices, so the mix is a compromise across 140 years); ruffs on a minority (about 10-20 %);
  long gowns for some older men (the populace's gown hint), short cloaks for some. Women in bodice and full skirt with a
  linen coif, some with a hat over the coif, aprons for many, a small ruff on a few. No farthingales or court dress.
- **Brentford 1016 soldiers:** a knee tunic in the side's tint, trousers and leg wraps; a rounded iron helmet for about a
  third, a soft cap for a quarter, bare-headed otherwise. No mail, shields or spears: the formation was staged without
  equipment and that is kept.
- **Barnet 1471 soldiers:** a padded jack in the side's tint (the two tints the code already uses, neither side's
  livery), hose, a sallet for most and a hood for the rest. No weapons, bows, badges or heraldry.
- **Children** wear the adults' garments of their period scaled to them (boys are shown breeched in the Tudor set; small
  boys in skirts are not modelled). **Skin:** the populace's skin value is used, now weighted towards the lighter end
  (the old even spread read, on figures with faces, as a far more varied population than the evidence for any period
  here suggests); the darker tones remain for a minority, as documented in Roman and Tudor London.

## Still wrong

- Close up (2 m) the figures read as mannequins: smooth plastic shading, faces without expression, eyes as painted
  geometry, no eyelashes or real hair strands; hair is a shell over the scalp. Not photorealistic.
- Garments are rigid surfaces: no cloth simulation, skirts and cloaks swing only as far as the thigh and pelvis bones
  carry them; a walking leg can cut through a cloak's edge; long skirts are stiff cones with shallow folds.
- Hems and openings are flat cut edges; jacks show no quilting, doublets no buttons, the sallet reads more like a round
  kettle hat; shoes are smooth shells; belts sit as plain bands.
- Coarse levels (beyond about 12 m) paint the shell garments onto the body, so sleeve ends and collars are jagged at
  that resolution, and level changes pop (no cross-fade).
- One gait for everyone of a body (no limp, no carrying, no turning except the populace's on-the-spot turn at the end
  of a piece); walkers slowing to turn shorten their steps, which reads as shuffling for a moment.
- Colours come from the old muted palette: many neighbours in similar browns and greys; linen coifs read very white
  in shade.
- Seated figures rest their hands on their thighs on the old block stool; groups face each other but do not interact.
- Animals, carts and boat crews are unchanged (still the simple shapes).
