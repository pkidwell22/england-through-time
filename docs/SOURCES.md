# Sources, dates and confidence

Generated from `data/` after merging the three research passes in `research/`. The data files are the authority; this is a readable index.

**Evidence classes.** *attested-standing / -excavated / -documentary / -pictorial*: existence, place and date are supported by fabric, excavation, documents or period images. *reconstructed*: existence attested, form is a reconstruction. *conjectural*: plausible fill-in, not individually evidenced. In every case the 3D form is a parametric stand-in; heights are almost never documented.

**Access caveat.** Research used automated fetching. Historic England (list entries and research records), parliament.uk, the main Historic Royal Palaces pages, the British Museum, UNESCO, Britannica, most Folger pages, ADS catalogue pages and long LAMAS PDFs refused automated access, and British History Online was unreachable during the Roman pass. Several standard references therefore could not be checked directly; figures seen only in secondary summaries are flagged in each entry's `uncertainty` or `dateNote`. Only URLs that were actually fetched are listed. Wikipedia was used for leads and coordinates, never as the sole support for a date, and is not listed. All coordinates are estimates from modern landmarks, not survey data.

## Where evidence is thin

- **All building heights.** Almost none are documented; every height in the model is an estimate.
- **Prehistory.** No pre-Roman settlement on the site of the City; the roundhouses are conjectural tokens. Tidal-head position before the Bronze Age and the woodland-cover percentages are illustrative.
- **Roman bridge.** No bridge of the 50s is dated; 'in place by 63' and 'built by c. 85–90' are inferences, and the pier-base identification is only probable. Form, deck height and end date unknown.
- **'Governor's palace'.** Function and unity disputed; western range hypothetical.
- **Huggin Hill baths.** Only 'late 1st century' verified; demolition date unverified.
- **Forum.** The c. 167 m square figure and the first forum's court size are unverified; elevations conjectural.
- **Roman population.** 20,000–30,000 at peak (institutional figures); no sourced 3rd–4th century figure.
- **5th–9th centuries inside the walls.** An evidence gap; scrub and the small enclave by St Paul's are guesses.
- **Anglo-Saxon St Paul's.** Form and exact site unknown; placeholder models.
- **Timber bridges c. 1000–1209.** Line, form and number of rebuildings unknown.
- **Southwark burh.** Documented, but no defences ever found (not modelled).
- **Baynard's Castle I, Montfichet's Tower, Savoy Palace before 1381.** No plans or images.
- **St Magnus, St Olave, Fishmongers' Hall.** Earliest dates and medieval forms uncertain.
- **Old London Bridge.** Overall length and arch count are firm; pier, arch, starling, chapel and gate dimensions and the deck height are not documented. Houses are generated.
- **North-bank reclamation.** Roman advance c. 35 m (secondary figure); no verified figure for the medieval advance.
- **Walbrook.** Covered piecemeal c. 1440–1550s; no single date.
- **Custom House.** The often-quoted 1559 rebuilding could not be confirmed and is not shown.
- **Savoy Hospital.** Only Stow's 'about 1509' found.
- **Globe and Swan.** Globe diameter and side count are extrapolations from a small excavated arc; the Swan's size is undocumented.
- **Nonsuch House.** No measured drawing; the Dutch prefabrication story is later tradition.
- **Lambeth Palace, Bermondsey Abbey.** Not covered by the research passes; dates unverified.
- **Population after 1600.** The 1625 figure is an interpolation.

## Not modelled, though documented

Paul's Cross; Whitefriars; Montfichet's Tower; the Blackfriars wall extension (1282–1310); the Tower moat; the Holbein Gate; the London Bridge corn mills (c. 1590–91); Bulmer's water engine at Broken Wharf (1594); Moorfields walks (1605–07); the Lundenwic ditch (840s); the Fortune, Theatre and Curtain playhouses and the New River Head (outside the modelled area).

## Research notes carried over

**Prehistoric and Roman**

- Year convention: signed historical integers, negative = BCE, no year 0. Where a phase 'from'/'to' is my bracket for a period-level statement (e.g. 'mid 2nd millennium BC', 'late 4th century') the dateNote says so; do not present those as measured dates.
- Verification status: every URL in 'sources' was fetched successfully during this research and supports the claim it is attached to. Entries marked 'lead' (Wikipedia) or 'secondary' are weaker and are flagged in the text. Anything described as 'my recollection', 'general knowledge' or 'not verified' is NOT backed by a fetched source and should be treated as conjecture until checked.
- Sources I could not access: all Historic England list entries and research reports (HTTP 403), British History Online (401), the ADS library catalogue pages (403), londonmithraeum.com (403), and most long LAMAS Transactions PDFs (downloaded but not machine-readable in this environment). The key unread primary works are: Bateman, Cowan & Wroe-Brown 2008 (amphitheatre), Shepherd 2012 and Howe & Lakin 2004 (fort), Marsden 1987 and Milne 1992 (forum-basilica), Marsden 1975 and Milne 1996 (palace), Marsden 1976 and Rowsome 1999 (Huggin Hill), Shepherd 1998 (Mithraeum), Watson, Brigham & Dyson 2001 (bridge), Sidell et al. 2000 (Holocene Thames), Drummond-Murray & Thompson 2002 and Cowan et al. 2009 (Southwark).
- Heights: no Roman building in this dataset has a documented original height except the city wall (c. 6.4 m to the wall-walk) and the Plantation Place fort bank (c. 3 m). The first basilica's 'up to 12 m' is an excavators' extrapolation. Every other height in a 3D model is reconstruction and should be flagged as such in the viewer.
- Coordinates are WGS84 to about 4 decimal places and are approximate site centres placed from published locations and general knowledge of the modern street plan, not surveyed points; the Mithraeum, palace, Huggin Hill and amphitheatre coordinates derive from gazetteer/listing data, the rest are estimates. Linear features (wall, riverside wall, waterfront, roads, bridge) need their own geometry.
- Building materials for the general fabric of the town: 1st-2nd century housing overwhelmingly timber-framed with wattle-and-daub or mudbrick walls (Regis House, London Museum); stone (Kentish ragstone with tile courses, flint in the first basilica) largely confined to public buildings, the fort, defences and later town-houses.
- Shoreline: the 1st-century north bank lay about 100 m north of the modern quayside, just north of Thames Street; Roman reclamation moved it south by a few tens of metres by the mid 3rd century; the riverside wall of c. 255-275 then fixed the town's river edge on the Thames Street line. The south bank had no fixed edge - islands and tidal channels.

**Saxon and medieval**

- Every URL listed under 'sources' was fetched successfully during this research and supports at least part of the entry. Wikipedia entries are marked 'lead only' and are never the sole source for a date.
- Sites that refused automated fetching (HTTP 403): historicengland.org.uk (list entries and research records), parliament.uk, whc.unesco.org, britannica.com and the main hrp.org.uk pages. stpauls.co.uk timed out and several mola.org.uk and londonmuseum.org.uk URLs returned 404. Figures that I saw only in search-result summaries from those sites (Westminster Hall 73 x 20 m; White Tower 36 x 32.5 m and 27 m; Winchester Palace gable 50 ft; St Paul's spire c. 400 ft per Schofield; quire of St Paul's complete 1148) are flagged in the relevant notes as not directly verified.
- Heights are the weakest data throughout. Only these are documented: White Tower 27 m; Greyfriars church 64 ft (Stow); Old St Paul's nave vault 93 ft and the disputed spire; Westminster Abbey vault c. 31 m. All other heightM values are null and any modelled height is conjecture.
- Stow's Survey (1603) is a late and sometimes muddled witness: his regnal-year conversions can be a year out (1436 for January 1437; 1282 for the winter of 1281-82) and his measurements (St Paul's 720 ft long, London Bridge 60 ft high, Westminster Hall 270 ft) are unreliable. Where modern archaeology or VCH differs, the entry follows the modern source and says so.
- Coordinates are WGS84 to about four decimals, taken from the known site of each monument; those for lost sites (Baynard's Castle I, Montfichet's Tower, Steelyard, Wool Quay, Lundenwic centre, Southwark burh) are approximate to roughly 50-100 m.
- Where an entry's phase has from equal to to, it is a single-year event. Null bounds mean unknown or open. 'Evidence' describes the best class of evidence for the item as a whole; individual phases may be weaker, as their dateNote explains.
- Not verified and deliberately left out or flagged: a fire at St Paul's in 675; the '1091 storm' destroying London Bridge; dendro dates for the first Saxon bridge; the 1434 date for Fishmongers' Hall; a 1067 first mention of St Magnus; any single figure for how far the north bank advanced; Lundenwic population estimates; a 1463 order to cover the Walbrook.
- Key open-access items consulted directly as PDFs: LAMAS Transactions vol. 50 papers on the Royal Opera House, Queenhithe and London Bridge (Archaeology Data Service), and two Historic Royal Palaces teaching documents. The definitive monographs (Watson, Brigham and Dyson 2001 on London Bridge; Gerhold 2019 on the bridge houses; Schofield 2011 on St Paul's; Schofield et al. 2018 on the waterfront) are not open access and would be the place to firm up the dimensional gaps listed above.

**Tudor and Jacobean**

- Coordinates are approximate WGS84 positions of the historical sites estimated from modern landmarks (4 decimals, roughly +/- 30-50 m); they were not taken from a surveyed dataset. Old London Bridge ran about 45 m east (downstream) of the present bridge, from beside St Magnus the Martyr to just east of the present Southwark abutment; positions of features on it are interpolated from pier numbers counted from the north (chapel 9th pier, drawbridge tower/Nonsuch House 13th, Stone Gate 18th of 19).
- Years are new-style historical years. Stow and the building accounts use old-style years beginning 25 March, so e.g. the Royal Exchange royal visit (23 January '1570') is 1571, the Banqueting House fire (12 January '1618') is 1619, and Henry VII's chapel foundation (24 January '1502') is 1503.
- Heights are almost never documented for this period; every heightM that is not null is either a recorded figure (Banqueting House, Greyfriars church, St Paul's tower per Hooke, Fortune contract storeys, 1581 banqueting-house masts) or flagged in the note as an estimate. Leave the rest to be styled by building type.
- Sources that could not be fetched (HTTP 403/404): Historic England list entries (Rose 1012707, Globe, Hope and bear gardens 1433271), Historic Royal Palaces pages (Tower, Banqueting House), British Museum and Folger catalogue records, the Royal Collection main site (the col.rct.uk mirror worked), London Picture Archive, and the Oxford Text Archive proclamation texts. Claims that would have rested on them are marked unverified.
- Wikipedia was used only as a lead for Gerhold-derived figures on London Bridge (house counts, storeys, chapel lease date) and for the Visscher/Norden panoramas; these are marked 'lead only' and should be checked against D. Gerhold, London Bridge and its Houses c.1209-1761 (London Topographical Society 2019; Oxbow 2021) before being treated as firm.
- Dissolution summary for the timelapse: Holy Trinity Aldgate surrendered 1531-32 and its church was razed in the 1530s; Charterhouse suppressed 1537-38 and became a mansion from 1545; Blackfriars, Greyfriars, Austin Friars and Whitefriars all surrendered 10-12 November 1538; St Bartholomew's and St Mary Overie in 1539. Churches demolished: Holy Trinity, Blackfriars, Whitefriars, St Bartholomew's nave, Charterhouse church. Churches kept: Greyfriars (Christ Church, 1547), Austin Friars nave (Dutch Church, 1550), St Bartholomew's choir, St Mary Overie (St Saviour).
- Things that must NOT appear by 1625: Inigo Jones's St Paul's portico (1633-42), Covent Garden piazza (1630s), the gap in the London Bridge houses (fire of 1633), the Rubens ceiling, Lincoln's Inn Fields development, the Queen's Chapel at Somerset House (1630s).
- One search result page contained embedded text instructing the reader to ignore prior instructions; it was disregarded and nothing from it was used.
