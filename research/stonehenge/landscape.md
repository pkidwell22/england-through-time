# Stonehenge landscape research: features around the stones

Years are signed (-2500 = 2500 BCE). Source ids resolve in `sources-landscape.json`. Machine-readable version: `landscape.json` (31 features, vegetation table, events).

**Reliability warning.** Several primary papers (Antiquity PDFs, Wessex Archaeology and Historic England PDFs, Historic England list entries, Southampton ePrints) were not readable (403 or binary PDFs). Much dimensional data therefore comes from Wikipedia pointers (`sto-wp-*`), English Heritage pages, press articles and abstract pages. Treat those figures as pointers to verify, not as final. Where sources disagree both figures are given. Nothing was cited from memory; items I know only from memory (e.g. the Coneybury Anomaly date) are flagged as unsourced.

## 1. Robin Hood's Ball causewayed enclosure
- Early Neolithic, "centuries around 3500 BC" (sto-eh-history); Wikipedia: probably built c. 4000 BC, possibly used to c. 3000 BC (sto-wp-robinhood). Recommended: from -3600, complete -3550, abandoned c. -3000, grassed ruin to 1625. Confidence low.
- Two ditch circuits with low banks behind; one causeway per Wikipedia (sto-wp-robinhood). Never comprehensively excavated. No diameter retrieved.
- Lat/lon 51.2128, -1.8550 (sto-wp-robinhood): about 3.8 km north and 2 km west of the provisional origin, **outside the provisional bounds**; just north of the WHS boundary.
- Show: fresh white segmented ditches and low chalk banks, then grassed.
- Unknown: size, internal features, end date.

## 2. Long barrows
- Internet Archaeology 47 lists WHS long barrows: Winterbourne Stoke 1 (labelled WS71 there), Wilsford 34, Winterbourne Stoke 86, Wilsford 41, Amesbury 14, Wilsford 30, Wilsford 13, Amesbury 10a and 7 (possible oval barrows), Amesbury 42, Durrington 76 (by the Cuckoo Stone), Amesbury 140 (sto-ia47-longbarrows). Exactly how many are certain is not stated (sto-ia47-index).
- **Amesbury 42** (east end of the Greater Cursus): 62 x 20 m, 0.29 m high now, ditch 11 m, N-S, ditch first causewayed then continuous; radiocarbon 3640-3370 cal BC (sto-ia47-am42). English Heritage cites an antler at 3520-3350 BC, dug by Thurnam 1866, and says whether cursus or barrow is older is unknown (sto-eh-reconstructed). Lat/lon 51.187683, -1.804781.
- **Winterbourne Stoke 1**: 84 x 27 x 3 m, NE-SW, side ditches 12 m, no date (sto-ia47-ws1). 51.172744, -1.858341.
- **Normanton Down**: Wilsford 30 is 43 x 20 x 2.3 m (51.168671, -1.838186); Amesbury 14 is 33 x 18 x 1.6 m (51.174873, -1.836305); no dates (sto-ia47-wil30, sto-ia47-am14). English Heritage says two small long barrows here; a Neolithic mortuary enclosure lies south of one (sto-eh-reconstructed, sto-wp-normanton).
- Dates: 4th millennium BC generally (sto-ia47-index); modelled c. -3700 to -3500 except Amesbury 42 (-3600 to -3500). Show white chalk mounds and ditches, then grassed.
- Unknown: "King Barrow" long barrow not found; no dates for most.

## 3. Greater and Lesser Cursus
- Greater Cursus: about 2.76-3 km long, about 100 m wide, up to about 150 m at the west; ditches uneven (west terminal ditch c. 2 m deep, 2.75 m wide; south ditch 0.75 m deep, 1.8 m wide at the top); three possible entrances (N, two S) (sto-wp-cursus, sto-eh-reconstructed). No bank dimensions anywhere.
- Dates: red deer antler in the west terminal ditch gave 3630-3375 BCE (sto-wp-cursus); Thomas et al. 2009 report an antler about a millennium older than the 1940s date 2890-2460 BC (sto-antiq-thomas2009). Recut c. 2500 BCE and 2000-1500 BCE (sto-wp-cursus). Recommended: from -3550, built -3500, persistent low ruin to 1625. Confidence medium.
- Terminals: Amesbury 42 beyond the east end; a Bronze Age round barrow inside the west end (sto-wp-cursus).
- Lesser Cursus: about 400 x 60 m, WSW-ENE, c. 750 m NW of the Greater Cursus west end, antler date c. 3000 BCE (Wikipedia only, conflicts with English Heritage's c. 3500); ploughed to a cropmark since WWII (sto-wp-cursus, sto-eh-history). Confidence low.
- Lat/lon Greater Cursus about 51.186, -1.826 (approximate).
- Surroundings: open chalk grassland by snail evidence (sto-eh-reconstructed).

## 4. Riverside monuments
**Coneybury**: Anomaly pit about 12 m NW of the henge, early Neolithic pottery, bone and flints; henge oval about 45 x 55 m, excavated ditch c. 4 m wide, 3 m deep, levelled by ploughing, probably medieval (ridge and furrow) (sto-wp-coneybury). No absolute dates retrieved; the brief's -3800 and -2700 are unverified. 51.1735, -1.8093.

**West Amesbury henge / "Bluestonehenge"** (51.1714, -1.7980): ring of stone sockets, up to c. 27 stones in a circle about 10 m across, stones removed 2469-2286 BC, henge ditch (about 25 m wide) and outer bank c. 2400 BC, stones believed to have been bluestones from socket shapes (sto-wp-bluestonehenge). Erection date uncertain (hoped c. 3000 BCE; Wikipedia range 3400-2500 BC). Confidence medium for the ditch and removal dates, low for erection.

**Cuckoo Stone** (51.18923, -1.79168): squat sarsen, erected before 2000 BC, a post stood in the socket first, cremations around 2000 BC, Roman rectangular building with coins to the SW, now lies fallen (sto-wp-cuckoo). Date of fall unknown.

**Woodhenge** (51.1894, -1.78576): six oval rings, 168 postholes, outer ring 43 x 40 m, ditch 2.4 m deep and up to 12 m wide, bank 10 m wide, 1 m high, 110 m overall, NE entrance, child burial at the centre, teenager in east ditch, ditch dated 2470-2000 BCE (sto-wp-woodhenge). Post heights (up to 7.5 m) are inferred. Modern concrete markers are not to be shown.

**Durrington Walls** (51.1925, -1.7875):
- *Settlement.* Eight houses excavated 2006, c. 16 ft (about 5 m) square, clay floors, central hearths, box-bed and dresser outlines, dated 2600-2500 BC in the 2007 release (sto-bristol-2007). Later Bayesian modelling: occupied only about 10-12 years, finished c. 2500-2460 BC, chalk floors renewed at most nine times (sto-archco-durrington). Total houses unknown; "up to 1000 houses/4000 people" (sto-wp-durrington) is an upper speculation; the release says "hundreds of people". Recommended: -2515 to -2480, a few dozen houses (conjectural count).
- *Timber ring under the bank (2016).* Up to about 300 posts about 5 m apart in a ring 440-450 m across, up to c. 5 m tall (Wikipedia says 7 m and 1.5 m diameter: conflict), lasting up to c. 50 years, then pulled and the sockets packed with chalk rubble (sto-archco-durrington, sto-wp-durrington). The 2015 "standing stones" claim was refuted in 2016 (sto-wp-durrington).
- *Henge.* About 500 m across, completed c. 2480-2450 BC along the post-pit line; bank about 3 m high (up to 30 m wide per Wikipedia), ditch about 5-6 m deep (Wikipedia 5.5 m deep, 18 m wide top, 7 m base); entrances N and S blocked 2-3 centuries later leaving one east entrance (sto-archco-durrington); Wikipedia lists NW and SE entrances (conflict) (sto-wp-durrington).
- *Southern Circle.* Four rings built c. 2600 BC with two more later (Wikipedia), or enlarged with about 200 posts when the palisade came down (Current Archaeology): conflict. Oriented to midwinter sunrise. Not replaced by stone in any fetched source.
- *Northern Circle.* A smaller timber circle just north, found by Wainwright 1966; no ring or post data (sto-wp-durrington). Conjectural form.
- *Shafts (Gaffney et al. 2020).* 20 anomalies in arcs, some 20 m or more across and at least 5 m deep, circuit over 2 km, Late Neolithic radiocarbon date from one pit (sto-ia55-gaffney2020). 2025 update: 16 pits or probable pits, diameters 14-20 m, OSL cores; Leivers 2021 argued some are natural sinkholes; the authors still support a human-modified pit structure (sto-ia69-gaffney2025). Wikipedia dates to about 2400 BC. Show only as an optional conjectural overlay.
- *Durrington Avenue.* About 170 m long, 27 m wide, stone-surfaced per the 2007 release, houses on both sides (sto-bristol-2007).

## 5. Stonehenge Avenue
- 2.8 km (English Heritage; Wikipedia "nearly 3 km"), parallel banks and ditches, corridor about 12 m wide, ditch midpoints about 30 m apart at King Barrow Ridge, outer ditches V-shaped about 2 m deep; four antler picks date construction to 2345-2200 BC; mostly levelled by ploughing; section under the A303 excavated 1967 (sto-eh-reconstructed). Banks sliced off but ditches visible in 2013 (sto-wp-avenue).
- Natural periglacial stripes lie under it; Parker Pearson holds the Avenue followed them (sto-wp-avenue).
- Recommended: from -2350, built -2300, levelled progressively. Confidence medium. Course coordinates held by other researchers.

## 6. Early Bronze Age barrow cemeteries
- Normanton Down: about 40 round barrows (bowl, bell, disc, one saucer), at least 35 surviving in 1957, burials roughly 2600-1600 BC; Wilsford G7 probably 2000-1800 BC, central mound 30 m, ditch 8 m (sto-wp-normanton, sto-eh-reconstructed). Bush Barrow: c. 1950 BC, 3.3 m high, 49 m overall (or 40 m wide), gold lozenge 184 x 156 x 1 mm, 51.17051, -1.83482 (sto-wp-bushbarrow).
- King Barrow Ridge: early Bronze Age round barrows (sto-eh-history); counts not retrieved. A square enclosure on the ridge may be a Neolithic mortuary enclosure, a square barrow or a sheepfold (sto-sarsen2018).
- Winterbourne Stoke Crossroads: the long barrow WS1 focus; round barrow counts in search snippets (17, 19, 22) conflict and are unconfirmed.
- Whole landscape: several hundred tumuli; Hoare and Cunnington excavated about 379 on Salisbury Plain, c. 200 near the stones (sto-wp-stonehenge).
- Amesbury Archer: c. 2340-2300 BC, grid SU16324043 (outside the site), gold hair ornaments (sto-wp-archer).
- Cursus barrows: only the one inside the west end of the cursus is attested (sto-wp-cursus).

## 7. Bronze Age field systems
Survey 2015-16 found early fields or enclosures in the eastern WHS, replaced by a major ditch system with two burials in it, and an infant in a palisade ditch in the west (sto-roberts2018). Dated only "Middle Bronze Age" in fetched sources. Co-axial form and Richards 1990 were not confirmed; extents unknown.

## 8. Later prehistory
- Vespasian's Camp (51.1739, -1.7931): Iron Age, 14 ha, bank up to 40 m wide and 6 m high above the ditch floor, ditch 10 m, counterscarp 18 m, defences 68 m wide; name from Camden, not Roman; medieval road over the hilltop; 18th-century landscaping; A303 cut the north in the 1970s (sto-wp-vespasian). Construction date not retrieved; modelled -700..-600 as a placeholder.
- Blick Mead (51.1775, -1.7878): a warm spring (11 C), pit dwelling, aurochs; Wikipedia dates conflict (c. 8000-4000 BCE vs 6250-4700 BCE) (sto-wp-blickmead, sto-wp-vespasian); pollen and DNA show partly open woodland with meadow clearings (sto-soton-blickmead2022).

## 9. Roman period
Little attested: a Roman building and coins at the Cuckoo Stone (sto-wp-cuckoo); late 4th-century coins and pottery at the stones (sto-wp-stonehenge); a probable large settlement near the Avon and a Roman graveyard near the Archer grave (sto-wp-amesbury, hedged); arable colluviation continues (sto-french2012). Roman roads and Durrington Walls activity not found.

## 10. Early medieval and medieval
- A decapitated man died 600-690 CE inside the monument (sto-wp-stonehenge).
- Amesbury Abbey (Benedictine) founded c. 979 by Queen Ælfthryth; refounded 1177 as a Fontevraud priory; dissolved c. 1540 and demolished (the brief says 1539; sto-wp-amesbury gives 1540, the abbey article no year); estate granted to Edward Seymour, Earl of Hertford, held by the Seymours to 1675 (sto-wp-amesburyabbey, sto-wp-amesbury). Later houses (1661, 1834-40) are after 1625.
- Domesday 1086: 111 households, eight mills; Amesbury a crossing of the London to Exeter road (sto-wp-amesbury).
- Medieval ridge and furrow over Coneybury (sto-wp-coneybury). Sheep-corn downland and roads: not documented in fetched sources.

## 11. River Avon
96 km long, from the Vale of Pewsey through Durrington and Amesbury to Salisbury (sto-wp-avon). Alder-hazel carr and sedge fringed the floodplain; channel sedimentation rose with clearance c. 800-500 BCE (sto-french2012). Chalk-stream character not documented in fetched sources.
