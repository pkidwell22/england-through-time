# Stonehenge: the monument, Mesolithic to 1625

Researcher report. Source ids refer to `sources-monument.json`. All BCE dates are calibrated. "Snippet-only" means I saw the claim only in a web-search summary and did not read the page; those claims carry no source id and are flagged. Primary evidence actually read: Darvill et al. 2012 (Antiquity 86) and Marshall et al. 2012 (EH Research Report 1-2012), both as PDFs whose text I extracted locally.

## 1. Mesolithic posts (before the timeline)

Four or five pits in the present car park, three possibly holding large pine posts, dated 8500-7000 BCE (found 1966-67); any link to the later monument is unknown [sto-eh-history]. Charcoal dates in the EH chronology report appendix run about 8800-7600 cal BC (partial read) [sto-marshall-2012-eh-chronology]. Starts before -4000, so show nothing, or a note only.

| 2 | 2620-2480 | 2760-2510 to 2470-2300 | Five trilithons, double bluestone circle (Q/R), sarsen circle, Altar Stone (tentative), Station Stones, south barrow, Slaughter Stone with D/E, Heel Stone | trilithons -2540, sarsen circle -2530 |
| 3 | 2480-2280 | 2405-2225 to 2300-2100 | Central bluestone circle, ditch re-cut, Avenue, pit beside Great Trilithon, Beaker burial in ditch, D/E removed | Avenue -2315 |
| 4 | 2280-2020 | 2210-2030 to 2160-1925 | Bluestone oval (c. 24-25) and outer circle (40-60) | circle -2165, oval -2060 |

Key modelled dates (95%) from the EH report [sto-marshall-2012-eh-chronology]: sarsen circle 2580-2475; sarsen trilithons 2585-2400 (93%); stonehole E 2470-2275; pit WA2448 2410-2005; bluestone circle 2275-2030; bluestone horseshoe 2205-1920; Z holes 2020-1745; Y holes 1630-1525; end of stone settings 1630-1340. Table 3 and the narrative differ because the table is a simplified "suggested dates" column; the Y/Z digging and the stone settings overlap Stage 5 at the 95% level. Atkinson's older scheme (2800-2100 etc.) and Cleal 1995 phases are superseded [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled].

Later work. Parker Pearson et al. 2020 book, 2021 Waun Mawn paper and 2022 reply: not read at source (snippet-only). Their effect is to argue for bluestones in the Aubrey Holes from c. 3000 BCE and a Welsh stone circle (Waun Mawn, 110 m, c. 3400-3200) dismantled for Stonehenge, which would put the first stones in Stage 1; Darvill 2022 "Mythical rings?" disputes this. Cremations: the Willis et al. 2016 "The dead of Stonehenge" (Antiquity 90) summary (snippet-only, PDF returned 403) gives 3100-2600 cal BCE for the remains, an AH32 cremation at 3030-2880, and 150-240 original burials as informed guesswork. EH research page: Aubrey Hole 7 yielded 50+ individuals buried c. 3000-2800 BC [sto-eh-research]; Wikipedia: 63 individuals, 50,000+ fragments from the 2013 analysis [sto-wiki-stonehenge]. None of these moves the stage dates by more than a few decades for our purposes.

## 3. Stage 1 (c. 3000 BCE)

- Ditch and bank: circular, c. 110 m overall; "not technically a henge" since bank is inside; ditch cut with antler picks, construction completed 2990-2755 BCE (95%), probably 2955-2830 (68%) [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. EH adds a smaller outer bank [sto-eh-description]. Counterscarp bank inferred to be spoil from the Stage 3 re-cut [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. Whether the ditch was dug in segments: not stated in anything I read (unknown).
- Entrances: main NE, smaller S [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. Widths: not found.
- Aubrey Holes: 56 in a ring about 5 m inside the bank, circumference 271.6 m (about 86.5 m diameter, derived), mean excavated hole 1.06 m wide, 0.76 m deep [sto-wiki-aubrey-holes]. 2008 re-excavation of AH7 revived Hawley's idea that they held bluestones; Parker Pearson says stones from c. 3000 BC [sto-wiki-aubrey-holes, sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. The cremated woman beside AH7 has 98% probability of predating the ditch, so the holes may be earlier [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled].
- Cremations: see section 2. Darvill: more than 150 originally, 64 other deposits mostly in the ditch (stages 1-3), ceasing in Stage 3 [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled].
- Timber (uncertain): S passage with post facade; three or more rectangular structures; a NE entrance building of 50+ posts, with a line of four postholes 20 m out [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. Only one independent date (posthole 1884, 2580-2450). Attested: postholes. Inferred: roofs, uses.
- Stones B, C, 97 presumed sarsen, tentatively Stage 1; 97 sat in a hollow possibly formed under a recumbent sarsen [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled].

## 4. Stage 2 (2620-2480 BCE)

Order: trilithons first, sarsen circle after, bluestone double circle between (scenario 1), or all in quick succession (scenario 2) [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled].

- Sarsen circle: 30 uprights, 30 lintels, outer diameter c. 29.6 m [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]; uprights about 4.1 m high, 2.1 m wide, 1.1 m thick, about 25 t; lintels about 3.2 x 1.0 x 0.8 m; lintel tops 4.9 m above ground [sto-wiki-stonehenge]; EH says 30 m diameter, "suspended 4 m" [sto-eh-stones]. Upright tops follow the slope so the lintel ring is level (snippet-only). Inner diameter: not found in any fetched source. Mortise and tenon with tongue and groove: Darvill notes timber techniques; tenon/mortise counts on bluestones given in section 5. Curved lintels: not confirmed in fetched text. Gap between stones 1 and 30 slightly wider; stone 11 narrower and shorter [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled].
- Trilithons: five, horseshoe about 13.7 m across, opening NE, up to about 50 t; smallest pair about 6 m, tallest about 7.3 m above ground (the page also says "almost 7.5 m", disputed) [sto-wiki-stonehenge]. Stone 56 about 6.7 m above ground and about 2.4 m buried [sto-wiki-stonehenge]; EH: 56 is the tallest sarsen [sto-eh-stones]. Heights of the middle pairs: not obtained. Date: antler in socket 53/54, 2585-2400 (93%) [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled].
- Bluestones: Q/R double circle, 50-80 stones, dumbbell sockets on the E, a single line on S and W (Atkinson: unfinished; Parker Pearson prefers single arc there; Darvill prefers erosion) [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled].
- Altar Stone (80): about 4.9 x 1.0 x 0.5 m, lies prone, about 6 t, stone 55 lies across it [sto-wiki-altar-stone, sto-eh-research]. Darvill: tentatively Stage 2, could be any date before the Great Trilithon fell on it [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. Original posture unknown. Provenance: Clarke et al. 2024 Nature points to the Orcadian Basin, NE Scotland (snippet-only; Nature page redirected to login), EH says at least 750 km and "not Orkney" [sto-eh-research]; a 2026 study (Pirrie et al.) weakens the NE Scotland origin [sto-wiki-altar-stone]. Treat provenance as unsettled; it does not change the model, which only needs a flat slab.
- Station Stones 91-94: rectangle just inside the bank, 91 and 93 survive, 94 in the north barrow, 92 in the south barrow/chalk floor [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. Dating uncertain (EH timeline puts them with the sarsens; Atkinson earlier) [sto-eh-history, sto-wiki-station-stones].
- Heel Stone 96 and stonehole 97: two scenarios (96 added 2 m SE of 97, a pair straddling the axis; or 97's stone moved to 96); ditch around 96 dug late Stage 2 or early Stage 3 [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. 96: unshaped sarsen, 4.7 m above ground plus 1.2 m buried, 77.4 m from centre, about 35 t (EH: 36 t+), leaning toward the circle [sto-wiki-heel-stone, sto-eh-stones]. Hole 97 found beside it in 1979 [sto-eh-stones].
- Slaughter Stone 95 with D and E: facade in NE entrance; E dated 2470-2275 (90%); D and E removed in Stage 3; 95 now lies flat, about 4.9 m long (Wikipedia main page) [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled, sto-eh-stones, sto-wiki-stonehenge].
- Sarsen source: Nash et al. 2020 Science Advances: 50 of 52 sarsens share chemistry, source West Woods, Marlborough Downs, about 25 km N (snippet-only, Science page 403). EH research page agrees (25 km, 15 miles) [sto-eh-research]; EH stones page says about 19 miles [sto-eh-stones]. A 2024 reanalysis (Hancock et al.) queried single-source claims but kept West Woods (snippet-only).

## 5. Stages 3-5

- Stage 3: central bluestone circle (arc of five stoneholes), ditch re-cut, D/E removed, pit WA2448 beside the Great Trilithon (2410-2005), Beaker archer burial 2340-2195, Avenue (2430-2200) with first 530 m straight on the axis then curving to the Avon, total about 2.5 km, corridor about 12 m wide [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled, sto-eh-description]. Bluestonehenge (West Amesbury, c. 25 stones) dismantled; its stones may have moved to Stonehenge (unproven).
- Stage 4: Q/R and central circles dismantled; oval of c. 24-25 stones inside the trilithons; outer circle of 40-60 stones between trilithons and sarsen circle. About 80 stones used in total; 43 survive as stones or stumps [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. At least 3 bluestones have tenons, 2 have mortise pairs, 2 tongue-and-groove (reused as lintels) [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled].
- Stage 5: Y/Z holes (Y 1630-1525; Z 2020-1745 per EH model; EH web page says 1800-1500), filled with windblown sediment [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled, sto-marshall-2012-eh-chronology, sto-eh-history]. Last prehistoric structural change. Carvings 1750-1500. Last dated activity at the stones: Roman-period coins/pottery (late 4th c.) and a 7th-century execution burial (see section 8).
- Bluestone sources: Preseli Hills, SW Wales, about 230-240 km [sto-wiki-stonehenge, sto-eh-stones]. Carn Goedog (spotted dolerite) and Craig Rhos-y-felin (rhyolite) quarries, quarrying c. 3000 BC per UCL/Southampton 2019 (snippet-only); sci.news snippets say 3400/3200 which conflicts, and I did not resolve it. Waun Mawn: EH says at least 6 stones removed there, at least 3 unspotted dolerites at Stonehenge probably from it [sto-eh-research]. Critics: Darvill 2022 (disputes the Waun Mawn link and the bluestone-in-Aubrey-Holes reading), Brian John (blog, opinion). Parker Pearson et al. 2022 reply (Antiquity 96) reasserts. I read none of these papers.

Completeness. Missing today: uprights 13, 17, 18, 20, 24 and 24 lintels (Darvill). Ashbee 1998 argued never completed; Darvill thinks robbed in historic times because sockets of 13 and 20 are recorded [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. Banton et al. 2014 reported 2013 drought parchmarks, from search snippets between stones 16 and 21, suggesting the circle was intended complete; the paper's abstract says only that parchmarks "provide more evidence" [sto-banton-2014-abstract]. Verdict: unresolved. The EH page notes the planned arrangement "was apparently never completed" for Y/Z [sto-eh-history].

## 7. Alignment

- Axis NE-SW, midsummer sunrise / midwinter sunset, shared by trilithons, bluestone settings, sarsen circle and the first 530 m of the Avenue [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. Avenue lies over natural fissures aligned on this axis (abstract only). Stukeley noticed the axis in 1720 [sto-wiki-archaeoastronomy].
- Sunrise is "somewhat to the left" of the Heel Stone and centred on the Avenue; midwinter sunset is visible through the central arches from the Avenue's far end [sto-skyatnight-astronomy].
- Intent: Ruggles favours midwinter; Parker Pearson cites Durrington Walls pig teeth (midwinter feasting) and allows midsummer; most scholars treat Hawkins's 1960s claims as over-optimistic; Station Stone lunar alignment doubtful [sto-skyatnight-astronomy, sto-wiki-archaeoastronomy, sto-wiki-station-stones].
- No fetched source gave a numeric survey azimuth. Snippet-only: an old text adopted 49 deg 34' 18'' for the Avenue; unattributed.
- My calculation (not a source): lat 51.179, obliquity 24.0 deg (c. 2500 BCE) versus 23.44 (today). Upper-limb sunrise with refraction: horizon 0 deg 48.2 vs 49.3; 0.5 deg 49.1 vs 50.2; 1.0 deg 49.9 vs 51.0. Geometric-centre, no refraction, flat: 49.55 vs 50.6. So the midsummer sunrise point was about 1 deg further north in 2500 BCE. Midwinter sunset is the mirror, 360 minus midsummer value for a flat horizon. The NE horizon elevation is unknown from my sources; ask the geography researcher for a DEM profile (I assume 0-1 deg). The lead's provisional 49.9 deg axis is consistent with this range but unsourced.

## 8. Later history (mostly unknown)

- Bronze Age: carvings of Arreton Down axes and daggers, 1750-1500 [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled]. Laser scan 2012 (Abbott and Anderson-Whymark) found 71 new axeheads plus the 44 known (115) and stone-working marks on nearly every stone; counts snippet-only, HE page 403, a 2025 paper reports more.
- Iron Age: Vespasian's Camp c. 700 BC; nothing recorded at the stones in what I read [sto-eh-history].
- Roman: late 4th c. coins, pottery, trench with bluestone slab and coin read as a grave, a large pit or shaft (SPACES 2008) [sto-eh-history, sto-wiki-stonehenge, sto-eh-research]. Darvill: bluestone breakage in Roman times, removal of at least seven oval stones "highly likely" Roman (his view; Atkinson dismissed a Roman army demolition).
- Early medieval: decapitated man aged 28-32, 600-690 AD per Wikipedia (a snippet disputes the range; "second half of the 7th century"). Low confidence [sto-wiki-stonehenge].
- Medieval: Henry of Huntingdon c. 1130, "Stanenges", stones "raised up like gates"; Geoffrey of Monmouth 1136, Merlin and Killaraus; Hardyng c. 1457 "somwat bowand" [sto-tudortreasures-accounts, sto-eh-history].
- Tudor: Folkerzheimer 1562; de Heere 1573-75 (first on-site view; content not read); R.F. engraving 1575 shows "leaning" [sto-eh-sources]; Camden 1586/88: three circles within a trench, some 28 ft high with lintels [sto-tudortreasures-accounts, sto-webb-1655-stone-heng]; Smith 1588; Saxton 1590 "Stonage".
- Jones: surveyed for James I in the 1620s (EH), published 1655 by Webb; the plan is an idealised Roman restoration, not a record [sto-eh-sources]. First known excavation in the centre by Buckingham in the 1620s [sto-eh-research].
- Stone robbing: Wikipedia says stones were taken for building until the 17th century; no dated event [sto-wiki-stonehenge]. Which stones were fallen or broken in 1625: unknown, except by inference below.
- After the timeline (for backward reasoning): Aubrey 1666 plan; 1797 fall of the SW trilithon 57/58 with lintel 158 (re-erected 1958) so standing in 1625; 31 Dec 1900 stone 22 (and its lintel) fell, re-erection 1901-1964 [sto-wiki-stonehenge, sto-eh-description, sto-eh-history]. Stone 55 and the Great Trilithon had fallen before the first 17th-century plans (Darvill) [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled].

## 10. Vegetation and land use

Little fetched. Stage 5: Y/Z holes filled by windblown sediment, likely from nearby cultivation; Bronze Age shift to field creation [sto-darvill-2012-remodelled, sto-eh-history]. Mesolithic pine posts imply pine nearby (inference). Otherwise grazed chalk grassland is the standard assumption and is unsourced here; defer to the landscape researcher.

## Attested vs inferred vs unknown (summary)

- Attested: ditch/bank and 56 Aubrey Holes c. 3000 BCE; cremations; sarsen circle design and trilithons c. 2580-2400; bluestone sockets (Q/R, oval, circle); Heel Stone ditch; Avenue; Y/Z holes; carvings; later finds.
- Inferred: order within Stage 2; Altar Stone date; Station Stone date; stone robbing; Roman horseshoe; stones in the Aubrey Holes; Waun Mawn link; sarsen circle once complete.
- Unknown: ditch/bank profile; entrance widths; inner circle diameter; middle trilithon heights; which stones stood or lay in 1625; dates of falls of 55, 54, 95 and others; Altar Stone posture and source.
